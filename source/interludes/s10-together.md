# Empty Hands

**Players:** all three together (Katy, Stig, Saphira), each voting on their own phone
**When (for Sam, not shown to players):** the cave pause after Matthew's question to Stig, before Elara speaks. Matthew rests and nobody has moved him yet. It ends before he stands up.
**Reading time:** about 10 to 12 minutes along one path.
**Votes:** the majority wins. On a three-way tie Hans decides, and says the tie line under the choice.

## T1. After

The green glow drains out of the cavern walls in slow patches, like water out of a cloth. What's left is grey: ash on the floor, ash in the air, and the dead husk of the Heart in the middle of it all, ticking now and then as it cools.

Matthew lies where he slid out of it, propped against a stone, wrapped in Stig's blanket. His hands rest on the lute in his lap. They are grey to the elbow, and they don't quite stop shaking.

Thorn stands at the edge of the light, bow in hand, watching the tunnel.

For a while nobody says anything. You are all still ringing from the fight. Somebody's knee hurts. Somebody's fingers won't open properly. You are all, every one of you, alive.

Hans looks round at the cave, at the cold, at the old man in the blanket. "Fire," he says.

## T2. Fire

Hans gathers dead root wood from the cave walls, where it came loose when the Heart died: black, dry, light as cork. [SAM?: Hans lights a small fire from dead root wood in the cave] He sets it down in a pile. He looks at the pile. He takes it apart and builds it again as a square. Then a cone. Then, after a pause, a different square.

"Fire. I know how," he says. "Theoretically."

He borrows Stig's tinderbox and strikes it six times. On the seventh a spark takes. A thread of smoke goes up, then a small, bad, determined flame. Hans sits back on his heels and watches it with the full attention of something that was built to watch things.

"It is working," he says. "I am also surprised."

The fire doesn't do much against the cave. But it is orange, not green, and it pops, and everyone moves a little closer to it without deciding to.

→ Continue (go to T2b)

## T2b. Around the fire

The circle sorts itself out the way circles do. Matthew in his blanket, closest to the warmth, because nobody would have it any other way. Hans on his heels by the wood, appointed keeper of the fire by himself. Thorn stays at the edge, watching the tunnel, but he has come one step nearer.

Somebody's boots steam. Somebody's hands, held out to the flames, slowly remember how to open. The ash keeps falling, but near the fire it lifts again in the heat and glows for a moment before it goes out.

It's the first warm thing in this cave. It isn't much. It's yours.

→ Continue (go to T3)

## T3. Matthew

Matthew watches the fire for a long time. The light moves over his face and finds every line in it.

"The first time I came back to the Hollow's door," he says, "I came with nothing in my hands."

His voice is still cracked from three days of playing. He doesn't raise it. You have to lean in.

"A year late, with nothing in my hands. Just me and a story about how sorry I was. They sent me away." He doesn't look at Thorn. Thorn looks at the floor. "Maybe it would have gone the same way with a gift. I'll never know. But you don't come to that door with nothing. You bring something, so they know you meant to come and weren't just passing." [SAM?: the Hollow expects a guest-gift]

He shifts the lute in his lap, carefully, as if it might break, or he might.

→ Continue (go to T4)

## T4. The gift

"Don't do what I did," Matthew says. "You're taking me home. Well. Near enough. So bring something."

He closes his eyes, not to sleep, just to rest them. Hans feeds the fire one more piece of wood, gravely, like a man dropping a coin in a dish.

You look at each other over the flames. Between you there's a bag of loot from half of Valeria, a bard, a cleric, a woman who speaks four languages and a construct in headphones. You should be able to come up with a present.

Each of you votes on your own phone.

What do you bring?

## T5a. The bottle

The bottle comes out of the loot: the fine wine, dusty, the wax on the cork cracked with age. Nobody has had the heart to drink it. Nobody has had the time.

Hans holds it up to the fire and squints at it as if he could read its age through the glass. Possibly he can.

"Old drink," he says. "Elves like old things. So do I." He hands it back with great care. "Nobody gives me wine."

Matthew opens one eye at the bottle and almost laughs. "That'll do," he says. "That'll more than do."

→ Continue (go to T6a)

## T6a. Wrapping it

Somebody finds a clean cloth, the only clean cloth in the cave, and you wrap the bottle in it. Somebody ties it off with a strip torn from a hem. It looks like what it is: a present from people who have just come through a fight and wanted to do this properly anyway.

You pass it round the fire. Each of you holds it for a moment. It is heavier than a bottle should be. That's probably just your arms.

"Who carries it?" Hans asks. Before anyone can answer: "I carry it. I have the best grip. Also it is for a door, and I am a little bit a door."

"You're not a door, Hans."

"I was part of a hotel," Hans says, with dignity. "Some of it stays."

→ Continue (go to T7)

## T5b. A song

"A song," Matthew says, and his eyes open properly for the first time. "Now that's a present."

He tries to lift the lute, can't, and lets it rest. "Every verse of that song starts the same way. *Foxglove bells over the sleeping roots.* That line you get for free. The rest you bring yourselves."

So: three lines, one from each of you. Katy's comes last, because the last line is the one that has to land.

Hans raises one hand. "I will provide the beat."

"No, Hans," says everyone.

Hans lowers the hand. "Noted."

**Each of you writes one line on your own phone.** Keep it short enough to sing. Stig and Saphira first, in either order. Katy, yours closes it.

→ Continue (go to T6b)

## T6b. Our verse

Katy reads the four lines out over the fire, once, slowly: the first one his, the rest yours.

*Foxglove bells over the sleeping roots,*
*{line1}*
*{line2}*
*{line3}*

Then she tries it on the tune, quietly, finding where the words need to stretch and where they need to hurry. It mostly fits. Where it doesn't, Matthew hums the fix without opening his eyes, and she takes it.

It isn't the best verse ever written. It's yours. You can hear each of you in it, the way you can tell whose boots are on the stairs.

Hans nods along, very slightly, on every beat. He is not providing the beat. He is just near it.

→ Continue (go to T7)

## T5c. Nothing but him

"Nothing," someone says. "Just him. We bring him home."

Matthew looks at you as if you've said something in a language he used to speak.

Hans nods. "Good. Truth is cheap," he says. "Also heavy." He considers the old man in the blanket, and the distance to anywhere, and the state of everyone's legs. "I will carry it."

"I can walk," says Matthew.

"Yes," Hans agrees, kindly, and doesn't move from his side.

→ Continue (go to T6c)

## T6c. Nothing dressed up

It's a strange thing to choose, and once it's chosen it seems obvious. Not a bottle. Not a song. The man they sent away once, brought back by people who came a very long way to find him. Grey to the elbows, and honest about it.

You talk it through over the fire: what you'll say at the door, and what you won't dress up. You decide not to dress anything up.

Thorn, at the edge of the light, has turned his head a little toward the fire. He says nothing. But for the first time since the Heart fell, he has stopped watching the tunnel.

## T7. The door

"One more thing," Matthew says. His voice is going. "When you get to that door, somebody speaks first. Decide it now. Don't stand there looking at each other."

The fire pops. A flake of ash drifts down into it, flares, and is gone.

Each of you votes again.

## T8a. Katy

Katy. Of course Katy. It's her blood behind that door, and her mother's home, and in a way her father's.

Matthew nods slowly. "Good," he says. "They'll look at you and see her. Let them. Say your name first, before anything else. Make them hear it."

Katy turns the words over. They taste like stepping off a ledge.

Hans nods once, firmly, as if he had suggested it.

→ Continue (go to T9)

## T8b. Stig

Stig. The healer, the one who has carried Matthew this far.

Matthew looks at the big man for a long moment. "Yes," he says. "They'll listen to someone with hands like yours." He doesn't explain what he means. He doesn't have to.

Hans reaches over and pats Stig's shoulder once, heavily, the way you'd stamp a document.

→ Continue (go to T9)

## T8c. Saphira

Saphira. Of all of you, she speaks Elvish best.

Matthew opens one eye at her. "They'll like that," he says. "Old words, said properly. They always did."

On Saphira's cloak, the eye looks at the fire and says nothing, which is what it usually says.

→ Continue (go to T9)

## T8d. Thorn

You look at Thorn.

He has kept that door for longer than some of you have been alive. It's his to open, or not.

He hears the silence and turns his head. He looks at the fire, at Matthew, at Katy. For a moment it seems he'll say something long.

He nods once. "Yes."

That's all. It's enough.

→ Continue (go to T9)

## T9. Last word

The fire is low now. Hans gives it the last piece of wood and watches it catch.

Matthew has started telling you something about the summer he met her, and somewhere in the middle of a sentence his voice runs out. His head tips back against the stone. His hands go loose on the lute. For one bad moment every one of you looks at his chest. It rises, and it falls.

He's asleep. Just asleep.

Hans looks at him, then at all of you, then at the fire.

"Good fire," Hans says. "Good crew. Bad cave." He turns his headphones down, a small click you all hear. "I will log this."

Nobody moves Matthew. Not yet.

## End A. The Old Bottle

The bottle sits wrapped by the fire, where everyone can see it. Now and then one of you glances at it, the way you'd check that a door is locked.

It's a small thing to carry to someone's door. It means: we meant to come. We weren't just passing.

Katy hums the new verse once more, under her breath, so she won't forget it. Then the rest of you are humming it too, badly, out of time, and nobody minds.

Matthew doesn't wake. But his fingers move on the strings, once, as if in his sleep he has found the chord.

## End C. Nothing to Hide

Your hands are empty, and you look at them, and they feel fine like that.

You will walk up to that door with the truth and a sick man, and nothing in front of either. Whatever they say, they'll be saying it to what's really there.
